For a long time, hardware companies competed by building better products-
Better specifications, Better performance, More features, Lower cost.

That model worked when technology advantages lasted for years. Today, they often last for months!

A feature introduced by one company quickly becomes standard across the category. Global supply chains have matured, manufacturing access has expanded, and competitors can replicate technical improvements faster than ever before.

According to McKinsey, product life cycles across industries have reduced by nearly 4x over the past 50 years.

This is fundamentally changing differentiation in hardware. Because when technical advantages become easier to replicate, products slowly begin competing on familiarity instead of originality. And eventually, categories start looking interchangeable.

We see this across appliances, consumer electronics, wearables, IoT products and lifestyle hardware. Specifications improve every year. But the emotional distance between products keeps shrinking.

This is where commoditization begins. Not because products are poorly engineered. But because customers stop experiencing meaningful distinction between one product and another.

Research in consumer behavior consistently shows that customers form first impressions within seconds, heavily influencing perceived quality, trust and purchase intent long before specifications are evaluated.

That means customers are evaluating far more than features-
How refined the proportions feel.
How precise the fit and finish looks.
How confidently materials are handled.
How recognizable the design language becomes.
How consistent the product feels across a portfolio.

This is one of the reasons brands like Apple, Dyson, Braun and Nothing continue creating preference even in crowded markets. Their advantage is not only technology. It is recognition.

At Iuova Design Company, one question we often discuss during product development is-
“Will this product still feel distinctive after the next five competitors enter the category?”

Because long-term brand value is rarely built through specifications alone.
It is built through memorability, consistency and emotional familiarity over time!
